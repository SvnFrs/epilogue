## 2. Premise challenge: you have described two products

They share one spine, but they have different centers of gravity:

- **Identity A — Personal museum.** "Previously On for your brain." A single-player
  cognitive save-state archive for media that demands active attention. This is what
  `vision.md`, the constitution, and the Phase 1 `src/` UI build.
- **Identity B — Community knowledge layer.** A place where a niche community's hard-won
  knowledge (the wiki links, the YouTube deep-dives, the synthesis) is structured at
  capture, attributed, evergreen, and retrievable in two years, instead of scrolling out
  of a Discord/Telegram channel forever.

The genesis shows you oscillating between these the entire time. "Grinding Gang" was a
**group tech-knowledge hub**. "RDR2 pickup" is **personal**. The docs resolved the tension
by sequencing it (personal first, community later). Your message today re-opens it by leading
with the community pain.

The CEO read: **Identity B is the bigger, more differentiated, more widely-felt problem.**
"Notion/Letterboxd for my own media" is a nice second brain in a crowded space. "The place
niche-community knowledge stops dying in Discord" is a category nobody owns. The good news:
**they are the same architecture.** Your multi-tenant-by-schema design means the personal
save-state is just the single-player mode of the community engine. You do not have to choose
forever. You have to choose the **wedge**: what you build and validate first.

## 3. Landscape — why the incumbents leave this open

- **Layer 1 (tried and true).** Discord/Telegram optimize for *now* (real-time chat,
  ephemeral). Reddit optimizes for *engagement* (recency buries old gold; weak internal
  search). Traditional forums are Web 1.0 (rigid, discussion-shaped, cold). Notion/Obsidian
  are blank paper (high setup friction). Letterboxd/Backloggd are catalog + rating (no deep
  knowledge, no community curation). Wikis have structure but no personal voice and no feed.
- **Layer 2 (new and popular).** Digital gardens, Substack, community platforms (Circle,
  Skool), second-brain tools. All either personal-publishing or chat-community, not both.
- **Layer 3 (first principles).** Nobody optimizes for: *active-attention knowledge that is
  structured at capture, attributed to a person, owned by the community, indexable, and not
  buried by recency.* Chat tools optimize for the present moment. Feed tools optimize for
  engagement. Forums optimize for threads. None optimize for "this is evergreen and findable
  in 2028."

**Eureka:** Epilogue is not a Notion competitor or a Discord competitor. It is the
**evergreen knowledge layer** that niche communities currently fail to keep inside Discord
and Telegram. The personal save-state is the single-player tutorial that earns the habit;
the community knowledge base is the multiplayer payoff.

## 4. The strategic fork (pick the wedge)

All three are reachable from the current architecture. They differ in what ships and gets
validated FIRST, which changes what the constitution centers on.

**A) Personal-first (as written).** Ship the single-player save-state museum now (current
Phase 2 Core Engine). Community stays Phase 3. Lowest risk, matches the constitution exactly,
slowest to the differentiated value, competes with Notion/Letterboxd in the meantime.

**B) Community-first (the reframe).** Make the niche-community knowledge hub the primary
product: sub-spaces, invite/contribute/read roles, evergreen shared archive. Personal
save-state becomes a mode inside it. Highest differentiation and biggest pain solved, but
the biggest build (auth, roles, multi-user UI) and hardest to validate as a solo user.

**C) Hybrid wedge (recommended).** Build the single-player engine fast (validates structured
capture + Digital Paper + save-state with you as user 1), but pull exactly ONE community
feature forward from Phase 2: a **shareable read-only sub-space** (the "Grinding Gang" link).
Smallest step that proves the community thesis without building full social. The constitution's
"multi-tenant by schema" already supports it; you ship one feature, not a platform.

**Recommendation: C.** It keeps your fast MVP path, validates the differentiated community
thesis with one feature instead of a rewrite, and avoids betting the whole build on full
multi-user before structured-capture is proven sticky.
